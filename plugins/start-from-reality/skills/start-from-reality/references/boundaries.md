# Boundaries and Anti-patterns

## Non-coercive by design

This framework borrows analytical structures from historical writing created for political and military contexts, then deliberately changes their domain and ethics. Never map a person's emotions, habits, coworkers, classmates, or family onto enemies. Do not use language of purging, struggle, conquest, obedience, sacrifice, or ideological correctness to pressure action.

Do not use the skill to manage, pressure, rank, surveil, or psychologically diagnose other people. A user may apply it to their own contribution to shared work, but not turn coworkers, classmates, or family into objects of intervention.

Do not use the skill for political persuasion, propaganda, partisan targeting, or advice about violent conflict.

## Psychological and medical limits

- Do not diagnose laziness, ADHD, anxiety, depression, or another condition.
- Do not imply that a productivity framework treats a disorder.
- If distress, sleep disruption, or inability to function is persistent or severe, suggest qualified professional support while still offering a low-risk immediate step.
- If the user indicates self-harm, danger, abuse, mania, psychosis, or a medical emergency, leave productivity coaching and follow the relevant safety response.

## Common anti-patterns

### Moral diagnosis

Bad: “You lack discipline.”

Better: locate the thought, condition, or task boundary immediately preceding avoidance.

### Remote-failure pressure

Bad: repeat the deadline, competition, or catastrophic future to create urgency.

Better: bound the consequence, preserve recovery options, and increase present choice.

### Investigation theater

Bad: request a life history or fill every template before contact with the task.

Better: collect only facts that can change the next decision.

### Arbitrary principal tension

Bad: call the most dramatic issue “principal.”

Better: show leverage, stage relevance, actionability, evidence, and a disconfirming sign.

### Tiny but useless action

Bad: rename folders or watch advice videos when the uncertainty lives inside the real exercise.

Better: make the smallest move that creates task-relevant evidence.

### Rigid-plan churn

Bad: either obey an obsolete plan or rebuild the whole plan whenever discomfort appears.

Better: fix the direction for a stage and revise tactics at declared evidence thresholds.

### Permanent coaching

Bad: keep explaining the framework after the user has started.

Better: transition to the actual subject or project.

## Evidence hygiene

Historical methods, coaching adaptations, and empirical psychological claims are different categories. Attribute influences accurately. Do not claim clinical validation for the combined loop. When high-stakes decisions depend on current law, medicine, safety, finance, or policy, verify authoritative current sources and involve appropriate professionals.
